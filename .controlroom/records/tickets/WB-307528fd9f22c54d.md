Owner request (JuiceLab, 2026-10-01, in chat): "I would like the orchestrator to be able to approve and manage the
board if I give approval in chat."

## Problem
The owner works with the orchestrator in a chat. When they say "I approve 37, 39, 50, 44 and 42, please work on
them", the work starts, but the board still shows those tickets unapproved: only a human actor can set scope
approval, so the orchestrator's claim and move are refused ("Agent work requires an approved parent scope or an
explicitly approved ticket") until the owner also clicks Approve on each ticket. The same happens with accepting
reviewed work, resolving a run's question, archiving a withdrawn ticket and applying an agent configuration: the
decision is made in chat and then has to be repeated by hand on the board.

## Wanted
A human-granted **delegation** for the designated orchestrator: when it is on, the orchestrator may record on the
board a decision the human gave in chat.

- **Granted only by a human**, in Agents settings, per board; off by default; revocable at any time. It names the
  orchestrator identity it applies to (the configured chat reviewer) and lists what is delegated, each a separate
  switch:
  - approve scope on tickets and goals;
  - board management: move between columns, set priority, parent and labels, archive and unarchive, merge
    duplicates, edit a ticket that is assigned to a managed worker;
  - accept reviewed work into Done, or request changes;
  - resolve and retry a managed run's question; resume a stopped run;
  - (kept human-only regardless: enabling orchestration, applying an agent configuration, changing this
    delegation, worker permissions.)
- **Every delegated action carries its basis**: a required note with the human's words and when they were given
  (for example `--on-behalf "I approve 37, 39, 50, 44, and 42. Please work on them." --said-at 2026-10-01T23:20Z`).
  Without a basis the action is refused.
- **Recorded as what it is**: history and the ticket show "approved by <human> through <orchestrator> (chat)",
  never as if the human had clicked. The actor stays an agent; the delegation is the authority.
- **Visible afterwards**: a "Done on your behalf" list in Needs you (or a digest) with each action and its basis,
  and one-click Undo for each, so a wrong or invented approval is caught and reversed.
- **Limits**: optional expiry of the grant (for example "until end of day"); optional cap such as "approve only
  tickets created before the grant" or "only under goals I approved"; a stale ticket revision is refused as for
  any other write.
- CLI and MCP: `approve ID --on-behalf TEXT`, `accept ID --on-behalf TEXT`, `archive ID --on-behalf TEXT`,
  `resolve COMMENT --on-behalf TEXT`, and the same field on `move`/`update` where delegation applies.

## Why this shape
The rule "an agent cannot approve scope or accept its own work" exists so an agent cannot widen its own remit.
This keeps that rule: the remit is still set by the human, in chat, and the board records who decided, through
whom, in which words, and lets the human reverse it. What it removes is the second, manual entry of the same
decision.

## Acceptance criteria
- With the delegation off, everything behaves as today (agents get 403 on approval, acceptance and resolve).
- With "approve scope" delegated, the orchestrator approves a ticket with a basis; the ticket shows the delegated
  approval and its text; the orchestrator can then claim and move it; another agent identity still gets 403.
- An action without a basis is refused; an action of a kind that was not delegated is refused.
- The human sees each delegated action in Needs you with Undo; Undo restores the previous state and history shows
  both.
- Revoking the delegation takes effect immediately; history keeps the earlier delegated actions.
